We explore the tunneling transport properties of a quantum dot embedded in an optical microcavity 
page=1 block=-1299693338 (x=116 y=234) width=390.000000 height=5.000000 fontname=JVAGHG+Corbel-Bold wordint.min=1 wordint.max=3
and coupled to a semiconductor?superconductor one?dimensional nanowire (Majorana nanowire) 
page=1 block=-1299693338 (x=116 y=245) width=379.000000 height=5.000000 fontname=JVAGHG+Corbel-Bold wordint.min=1 wordint.max=3
hosting Majorana zero modes (MZMs) at their edges. Conductance profiles reveal that strong 
page=1 block=-1299693338 (x=116 y=256) width=362.000000 height=5.000000 fontname=JVAGHG+Corbel-Bold wordint.min=1 wordint.max=3
light?matter coupling can be employed to distinguish between the cases of highly nonlocal MZMs, 
page=1 block=-1299693338 (x=116 y=267) width=382.000000 height=5.000000 fontname=JVAGHG+Corbel-Bold wordint.min=1 wordint.max=3
overlapped MZMs and MZMs with less degree of nonlocal feature. Moreover, we show that it is 
page=1 block=-1299693338 (x=116 y=278) width=369.000000 height=5.000000 fontname=JVAGHG+Corbel-Bold wordint.min=1 wordint.max=3
possible to access the degree of Majorana nonlocality (topological quality factor) by changing the dot

spectrum through photon?induced transitions tuned by an external pump applied to the microcavity.

Over the past decade, a huge effort in both theoretical and experimental fields has been performing in the quest 
page=1 block=-1253986352 (x=116 y=329) width=400.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
for an unquestionable signature of exotic ‘half-fermionic’ states, the so-called Majorana zero-modes (MZMs) in 
page=1 block=-1253986352 (x=116 y=339) width=400.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
quasi-one dimensional hybrid semiconductor-superconductor nanowires with strong spin-orbit coupling sub-
page=1 block=-1253986352 (x=116 y=349) width=398.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
ject to external magnetic field, termed as Majorana nanowires1–5. In such devices, each isolated ‘half-fermionic’ 
page=1 block=-1253986352 (x=116 y=358) width=400.000000 height=7.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
MZM appears at one of the opposite nanowire ends when the bulk of the system undergoes a topological phase 
page=1 block=-1253986352 (x=116 y=369) width=400.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
transition6–8.

In tunneling spectroscopy measurements performed through a Majorana nanowire4,8–15, the emergence of 
page=1 block=-1253986352 (x=128 y=388) width=388.000000 height=7.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
a quantized zero-bias peak (ZBP) robust to changing of relevant system parameters such as magnetic field and 
page=1 block=-1253986352 (x=116 y=399) width=400.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
gate voltages is considered as a strong evidence supporting the emergence of the isolated MZMs. However, it 
page=1 block=-1253986352 (x=116 y=409) width=400.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
has been extensively demonstrated that other physical mechanisms such as the formation of trivial zero-energy 
page=1 block=-1253986352 (x=116 y=419) width=400.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
Andreev bound state (ABS) at wire ends due to inhomogeneous smooth confining potentials16–25 and disorder-
page=1 block=-1253986352 (x=116 y=428) width=398.000000 height=7.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
induced bound states22,25–27 can produce a robust quantized ZBP, leading to an ambiguity of the MZM signature.

The emergence of a trivial zero-energy ABS can be mathematically described as resulting from two half-
page=1 block=-1253986352 (x=128 y=449) width=386.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
fermionic states with some spatial separation between them. In this scenario, a quantized ZBP may arise if one 
page=1 block=-1253986352 (x=116 y=459) width=400.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
of these half-fermionic states couples to a tunneling spectroscopy probe stronger than the other15,20,21. This kind 
page=1 block=-1253986352 (x=116 y=468) width=400.000000 height=7.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
of trivial ABS formed by two half-fermionic states with small spatial separation between them has been dubbed 
page=1 block=-1253986352 (x=116 y=479) width=400.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
as partially separated ABS (ps-ABS)20 or quasi-Majoranas (quasi-MZM)21. Despite some recent advances, dis-
page=1 block=-1253986352 (x=116 y=488) width=398.000000 height=7.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
tinguishing ZBPs resulting from genuine topological MZMs, trivial quasi-MZMs and disorder still remains a 
page=1 block=-1253986352 (x=116 y=499) width=400.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
challenge15,22–25.
