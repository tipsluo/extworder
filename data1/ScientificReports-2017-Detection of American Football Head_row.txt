page=5 block=-1246492060 (x=116 y=75) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
guidelines39, sample rate is suggested to be six times the signal bandwidth. With a sample rate of 1000 Hz, we

expected that the linear acceleration signals above approximately 200 Hz to be undersampled, and chose an upper 
page=5 block=-1246492060 (x=116 y=95) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4

Wavelet Transform Features.  Wavelet Transform (WT) of a signal is the representation of the signal in real space 
page=5 block=-1246482720 (x=116 y=125) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
as a linear combination of wavelet basis functions, which are localized in both frequency and time. The main 
page=5 block=-1246482720 (x=116 y=135) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
advantage of WT is that it provides not only the power spectral density at different frequencies but also presents 
page=5 block=-1246482720 (x=116 y=145) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
quantitative information about their time dependence. Morlet WT was performed on the linear acceleration 
page=5 block=-1246482720 (x=116 y=155) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
and angular acceleration signals in each anatomically aligned directional component and vector magnitude. For 
page=5 block=-1246482720 (x=116 y=165) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
each of these, the frequency, value, and timing relative to the time-domain peak of the peak WT amplitude were 
page=5 block=-1246482720 (x=116 y=175) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
extracted. In addition, we estimated the bandwidth of the WT by calculating the frequency at which the peak 
page=5 block=-1246482720 (x=116 y=185) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
WT amplitude dropped below 10% of the maximum value. Similar to the PSD features, we extracted peak WT 
page=5 block=-1246482720 (x=116 y=195) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
amplitudes over time of linear acceleration and angular acceleration in each directional component and in vector 
page=5 block=-1246482720 (x=116 y=205) width=400.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
magnitude at frequencies from 10 Hz–200 Hz and 10 Hz–180 Hz, respectively, in intervals of 10 Hz.

Biomechanical Modeling Features.  We estimated kinematics of the neck and torso based on a 2-linkage biome-
page=5 block=-1246431944 (x=116 y=236) width=399.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
chanical model of the head-neck-torso. The kinematic model comprises a head rigid body attached to a neck 
page=5 block=-1246431944 (x=116 y=246) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
rigid body via a two-rotational-degree-of-freedom joint (universal joint). The axes of rotation for the universal 
page=5 block=-1246431944 (x=116 y=256) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
joint are the left/right axis (representing the atlanto-occipital joint) and the inferior/superior axis (representing 
page=5 block=-1246431944 (x=116 y=266) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
the atlanto-axial joint). The neck rigid body is further constrained such that its base (representing its attachment 
page=5 block=-1246431944 (x=116 y=276) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
to the torso) has a minimal distance with its position at time t = 0. This minimization of distance constraint rep-
page=5 block=-1246431944 (x=116 y=286) width=399.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
resents our assumption of a massive torso that has less motion than the less massive head. From this, we could 
page=5 block=-1246431944 (x=116 y=296) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
define the orientation of the neck with three degrees of freedom rotation. Then, given we have six degrees of 
page=5 block=-1246431944 (x=116 y=306) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
freedom knowledge of the head position (integrated from linear acceleration and angular velocity measurements 
page=5 block=-1246431944 (x=116 y=316) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
from the mouthguard), we could solve for the universal joint angles at the head-neck joint, the ball joint angles 
page=5 block=-1246431944 (x=116 y=326) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
of the neck, and the distance between the current position of the torso end and the initial position of the torso 
page=5 block=-1246431944 (x=116 y=336) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
end. Thus, we had a fully determined system with six knowns (head degrees of freedom) and six unknowns 
page=5 block=-1246431944 (x=116 y=346) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
(two head-neck angles, three neck angles, and one minimum distance constraint). For the model, we used a 
page=5 block=-1246431944 (x=116 y=356) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
neck length of 10.8 cm40, and a head centre-of-gravity location of 5 cm above the head-neck joint41. From this 
page=5 block=-1246431944 (x=116 y=365) width=401.000000 height=7.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
model, we extracted several features. First, we extracted the percent time over the 100 ms duration that the angles 
page=5 block=-1246431944 (x=116 y=376) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
between the head and neck linkages matched signs with the angles between the torso and neck linkages. This 
page=5 block=-1246431944 (x=116 y=386) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
represented a continuous positive or negative rotation of the cervical spine. Next, we extracted the peak absolute 
page=5 block=-1246431944 (x=116 y=396) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
joint angles, torso, neck, and head displacements (as measured at the base of the neck, base of the head, and 
page=5 block=-1246431944 (x=116 y=406) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
centre of mass of the head respectively), and respective velocities and accelerations as a representation of the 
page=5 block=-1246431944 (x=116 y=416) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
total motion of our model. Although the features extracted from the kinematics signals used untransformed 
page=5 block=-1246431944 (x=116 y=426) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
linear accelerations to avoid cross-coupling of linear and angular features, the model-based features used linear 
page=5 block=-1246431944 (x=116 y=436) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
accelerations transformed to the centre of mass of the head to estimate head velocities and displacements. Finally, 
page=5 block=-1246431944 (x=116 y=446) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
we extracted ratios of linear and angular acceleration kinematics, which represented the biomechanical coupling 
page=5 block=-1246431944 (x=116 y=456) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
due to the presence of the neck. Specifically, we took the ratios between the angular acceleration about each ana-
page=5 block=-1246431944 (x=116 y=466) width=399.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
tomical axis with respect to linear acceleration along the remaining two normal anatomical directions. The ratio 
page=5 block=-1246431944 (x=116 y=476) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
was computed for each time sample in each impact, and the mean, standard deviation, and peak were all utilized 
page=5 block=-1246431944 (x=116 y=486) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
as features.

Discussion
page=11 block=380109528 (x=116 y=104) width=50.000000 height=7.000000 fontname=CQFEGY+Corbel-Bold2 wordint.min=1 wordint.max=23
Here we present a study where we trained and validated a head impact classifier with collegiate field data. We 
page=11 block=-1246489430 (x=116 y=115) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
combined the use of a head impact sensor with independent video analysis to provide training and validation 
page=11 block=-1246489430 (x=116 y=125) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
data from collegiate football for the head impact classifier. Using this methodology, we have identified features 
page=11 block=-1246489430 (x=116 y=135) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
that were helpful in distinguishing between helmeted football head impacts and nonimpacts on the collegiate 
page=11 block=-1246489430 (x=116 y=145) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
football field.
page=11 block=-1246489430 (x=116 y=155) width=47.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
Classifier Performance.  In our collegiate field data, the majority of the data recordings had low IR readings 
page=11 block=-1246428920 (x=116 y=175) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
(73% of the nonimpacts and 23% of the recordings during helmet contact periods were low IR, or off-teeth), indi-
page=11 block=-1246428920 (x=116 y=185) width=399.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
cating that the mouthguard was likely removed from the teeth during these recordings. Since football is a unique 
page=11 block=-1246428920 (x=116 y=195) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
sport where not all players are always in action, device removal could occur frequently especially when the player 
page=11 block=-1246428920 (x=116 y=205) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
is idle and not in play. Since the amount of play time also varies from player to player and from event to event, the 
page=11 block=-1246428920 (x=116 y=215) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
number of off-teeth recordings could vary accordingly. Thus, the IR sensing mechanism is helpful to easily reject 
page=11 block=-1246428920 (x=116 y=225) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
such off-teeth recordings. For our application, the proximity sensing method needs to be specific for detecting 
page=11 block=-1246428920 (x=116 y=235) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
whether the device is mounted on teeth, instead of just detecting the device being in mouth. Extending to other 
page=11 block=-1246428920 (x=116 y=245) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
wearable head impact sensors, we advise that sensors include a suitable proximity sensing mechanism to sense 
page=11 block=-1246428920 (x=116 y=255) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
device placement and reject recordings when the device is not properly mounted.
page=11 block=-1246428920 (x=116 y=265) width=289.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
The SVM classifier helped to reject high-IR nonimpacts. Such recordings could occur when the player bites 
page=11 block=-1246428920 (x=128 y=275) width=388.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
or chews the mouthguard while it is worn, or when other objects may be present in the mouthguard tray to 
page=11 block=-1246428920 (x=116 y=285) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
generate a high IR reading (e.g. lip or finger). We trained two SVM classifiers using our collegiate football data, 
page=11 block=-1246428920 (x=116 y=295) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
with one optimizing for area under the ROC curve and the other optimizing for the F-measure. For both opti-
page=11 block=-1246428920 (x=116 y=305) width=399.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
mization schemes, the first features chosen during the forward feature selection process were low-frequency 
page=11 block=-1246428920 (x=116 y=315) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
PSD or WT features (LR linear acceleration PSD at 10 Hz for AUC and IS linear acceleration WT at 20 Hz for 
page=11 block=-1246428920 (x=116 y=325) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
F-measure). Optimization for AUC is often used for problems where ranking of class probabilities is important 
page=11 block=-1246428920 (x=116 y=335) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
and has been shown to be a superior metric than accuracy42, while optimization for the F-measure is usually 
page=11 block=-1246428920 (x=116 y=344) width=401.000000 height=7.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
helpful for detecting the rare class in unbalanced data43. In our data, after filtering with the IR signal, the num-
page=11 block=-1246428920 (x=116 y=354) width=399.000000 height=7.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
bers of impacts and nonimpacts are approximately balanced, in which case the F-measure may be less critical. 
page=11 block=-1246428920 (x=116 y=365) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
On the other hand, optimizing for AUC may help to minimize the overlap of classes at the decision boundary 
page=11 block=-1246428920 (x=116 y=375) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
in the transformed data space. In our study, the F-measure classifier had better cross-validation performance 
page=11 block=-1246428920 (x=116 y=385) width=400.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
on the training data, while the AUC classifier had better and more consistent performance on the test data. This 
page=11 block=-1246428920 (x=116 y=395) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
indicates that the F-measure classifier likely overfit to the collegiate football training and validation data. The 
page=11 block=-1246428920 (x=116 y=405) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
AUC classifier had consistent performance on the collegiate training dataset as well as the independent youth 
page=11 block=-1246428920 (x=116 y=415) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
test dataset. Based on this limited dataset, retraining of the classifier for youth football may not be necessary. To 
page=11 block=-1246428920 (x=116 y=425) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
evaluate this assumption, future studies using a larger youth dataset that includes all positions and more exten-
page=11 block=-1246428920 (x=116 y=435) width=399.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
sive video data are warranted.
page=11 block=-1246428920 (x=116 y=445) width=106.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
The rigorous video verification of ground truth labels helped to minimize noise in the ground truth training 
page=11 block=-1246428920 (x=128 y=455) width=389.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
data. As a result, we only trained the classifier using a small fraction of all recorded data that could be verified and 
page=11 block=-1246428920 (x=116 y=465) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
labelled through video analysis. Recognizing that, we also performed a qualitative evaluation of the classifier’s 
page=11 block=-1246428920 (x=116 y=475) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
ability to classify all data recordings, as shown in Fig. 8. The training data for the classifier only incorporated 
page=11 block=-1246428920 (x=116 y=485) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
about 10% of the data from this event. As expected, most of the recordings had a low IR reading (84%), and only 
page=11 block=-1246428920 (x=116 y=495) width=401.000000 height=6.000000 fontname=MinionPro-Regular wordint.min=2 wordint.max=4
a small proportion of the recordings (4%) among the 1219 recordings were classified as helmet impacts, which

Conclusion
