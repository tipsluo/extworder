Collisions between complex nuclei may give rise to their total or partial fusion. The latter case is found
page=1 block=106113013 (x=65 y=152) width=390 height=6 fontname=CKKFNF+AdvOT483a8203 wordint.min=1 wordint.max=5
experimentally to gain importance when one of the colliding nuclei is weakly bound. It has been commonly
page=1 block=106113013 (x=55 y=164) width=400 height=6 fontname=CKKFNF+AdvOT483a8203 wordint.min=1 wordint.max=4
assumed that the partial fusion mechanism is a two-step process, whose first step is the dissociation of the
page=1 block=106113013 (x=55 y=176) width=400 height=6 fontname=CKKFNF+AdvOT483a8203 wordint.min=1 wordint.max=5
weakly bound nucleus, followed by the capture of one of the fragments. To assess this interpretation, we
page=1 block=106113013 (x=55 y=188) width=400 height=6 fontname=CKKFNF+AdvOT483a8203 wordint.min=1 wordint.max=5
present the first implementation of the three-body model of inclusive breakup proposed in the 1980s by
page=1 block=106113013 (x=55 y=200) width=400 height=6 fontname=CKKFNF+AdvOT483a8203 wordint.min=1 wordint.max=5
Austern et al. [Phys. Rep. 154, 125 (1987)] that accounts for both the direct, one-step, partial fusion and the
page=1 block=106113013 (x=55 y=212) width=400 height=6 fontname=CKKFNF+AdvOT483a8203 wordint.min=1 wordint.max=4
two-step mechanism proceeding via the projectile continuum states. Contrary to the widely assumed
page=1 block=106113013 (x=55 y=224) width=400 height=6 fontname=CKKFNF+AdvOT483a8203 wordint.min=1 wordint.max=6
picture, we find that, at least for the investigated cases, the partial fusion is largely dominated by the direct
page=1 block=106113013 (x=55 y=236) width=400 height=6 fontname=CKKFNF+AdvOT483a8203 wordint.min=1 wordint.max=4
capture from the projectile ground state.

Introduction.—The understanding of fusion in collisions

example, in the 1930s, Oppenheimer and Phillips [6] tried

to explain the excess of protons in sub-Coulomb deuteron-

induced reactions by invoking a partial absorption mecha-
