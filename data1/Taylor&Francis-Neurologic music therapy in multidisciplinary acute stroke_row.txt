Background: There is increasing evidence for music-based interventions in neurorehabilitation,
page=2 block=1069163325 (x=13 y=168) width=372 height=5 fontname=HAMMBM+AdvOT46dcae81 wordint.min=1 wordint.max=5
improving mood and functional outcomes. In response, there is growing interest from health-care
page=2 block=1069163325 (x=13 y=178) width=372 height=5 fontname=HAMMBM+AdvOT46dcae81 wordint.min=1 wordint.max=4
providers in setting up Neurologic Music Therapy (NMT) services. This paper presents some
page=2 block=1069163325 (x=13 y=188) width=372 height=5 fontname=HAMMBM+AdvOT46dcae81 wordint.min=1 wordint.max=6
preliminary data on the feasibility and acceptability of NMT in the acute stroke, multidisciplinary
page=2 block=1069163325 (x=13 y=198) width=372 height=5 fontname=HAMMBM+AdvOT46dcae81 wordint.min=1 wordint.max=5
team setting, about which little is known.
page=2 block=1069163325 (x=13 y=208) width=160 height=5 fontname=HAMMBM+AdvOT46dcae81 wordint.min=1 wordint.max=5
Objectives: To assess the feasibility and acceptability of a two-day per-week NMT service over
page=2 block=1069163325 (x=13 y=218) width=372 height=5 fontname=HAMMBM+AdvOT46dcae81 wordint.min=1 wordint.max=6
24 months.
page=2 block=1069163325 (x=13 y=228) width=42 height=5 fontname=HAMMBM+AdvOT46dcae81 wordint.min=1 wordint.max=4
Methods: Data were collected on the number of referrals received, sessions attended, sessions
page=2 block=1069163325 (x=13 y=238) width=372 height=5 fontname=HAMMBM+AdvOT46dcae81 wordint.min=1 wordint.max=6
declined and reasons why. Staff completed questionnaires, and collected them from patients and
page=2 block=1069163325 (x=13 y=248) width=372 height=5 fontname=HAMMBM+AdvOT46dcae81 wordint.min=1 wordint.max=5
their relatives, rating interventions: 1. Not helpful, 2. Quite helpful, 3. Helpful, 4. Very helpful.
page=2 block=1069163325 (x=13 y=258) width=372 height=5 fontname=HAMMBM+AdvOT46dcae81 wordint.min=1 wordint.max=6
Patients completed the Visual Analogue Mood Scale (VAMS) pre-/post- a single session.
page=2 block=1069163325 (x=13 y=268) width=337 height=5 fontname=HAMMBM+AdvOT46dcae81 wordint.min=1 wordint.max=5
Results: Of 201 patients referred, 177 received treatment and 675 sessions were delivered.
page=2 block=1069163325 (x=13 y=278) width=372 height=5 fontname=HAMMBM+AdvOT46dcae81 wordint.min=1 wordint.max=7
Twenty-four patients were discharged before sessions were scheduled and 28 sessions were
page=2 block=1069163325 (x=13 y=288) width=372 height=5 fontname=HAMMBM+AdvOT46dcae81 wordint.min=1 wordint.max=7
declined, predominantly due to fatigue. Mean scores (SD) from questionnaire data were: patients
page=2 block=1069163325 (x=13 y=298) width=372 height=5 fontname=HAMMBM+AdvOT46dcae81 wordint.min=1 wordint.max=5
(n = 99) 3.34 (0.825), relatives (n = 13) 3.83 (0.372), staff (n = 27) 3.85 (0.388). Mean, post-session
page=2 block=1069163325 (x=13 y=308) width=372 height=5 fontname=HAMMBM+AdvOT46dcae81 wordint.min=1 wordint.max=5
VAMS data (n = 52) showed a non-significant reduction in ‘Sad’ (7.5, p = .007, CI = 2.1, 12.9) and an
page=2 block=1069163325 (x=13 y=318) width=372 height=5 fontname=HAMMBM+AdvOT46dcae81 wordint.min=1 wordint.max=4
increase in ‘Happy’ (+ 6.2, p = .013, CI = ?11.0, ?1.4).
page=2 block=1069163325 (x=13 y=328) width=204 height=5 fontname=HAMMBM+AdvOT46dcae81 wordint.min=1 wordint.max=5
Conclusions: Data suggest the service was feasible and helpful, particularly for patient mood,
page=2 block=1069163325 (x=13 y=338) width=372 height=5 fontname=HAMMBM+AdvOT46dcae81 wordint.min=1 wordint.max=6
possibly improving engagement in rehabilitation. Research to determine generalizability in different
page=2 block=1069163325 (x=13 y=348) width=372 height=5 fontname=HAMMBM+AdvOT46dcae81 wordint.min=1 wordint.max=4
stroke environments and treatment effects within them is warranted.
