Abstract: A solute transport model has been added to the WinSRFR surface irrigation software package. The model uses advective-
page=2 block=2084920982 (x=1 y=91) width=528 height=6 fontname=LPPLBM+AdvOT483a8203 wordint.min=1 wordint.max=6
dispersive principles to predict the flow of solute with the irrigation stream and its final longitudinal distribution along the field based
page=2 block=2084920982 (x=1 y=104) width=528 height=5 fontname=LPPLBM+AdvOT483a8203 wordint.min=1 wordint.max=6
on water flow predictions generated by the simulation engine. Computations are based on the fractional stepping method: advection is
page=2 block=2084920982 (x=1 y=116) width=528 height=5 fontname=LPPLBM+AdvOT483a8203 wordint.min=1 wordint.max=6
computed using the method of characteristics from the velocity field computed by the simulation engine water flow calculations, and
page=2 block=2084920982 (x=1 y=128) width=528 height=5 fontname=LPPLBM+AdvOT483a8203 wordint.min=1 wordint.max=6
dispersion is calculated from the advection results using a centered-difference finite-difference scheme. The Akima interpolation scheme
page=2 block=2084920982 (x=1 y=140) width=528 height=5 fontname=LPPLBM+AdvOT483a8203 wordint.min=1 wordint.max=5
was used to build the method-of-characteristics solution. The computations are robust and execute in little time. The accuracy of the model
page=2 block=2084920982 (x=1 y=152) width=528 height=5 fontname=LPPLBM+AdvOT483a8203 wordint.min=1 wordint.max=4
was evaluated with numerical tests and by comparison with field measurements. Results suggest that the model can generate useful

predictions under typical irrigation conditions. DOI: 10.1061/(ASCE)IR.1943-4774.0001507. © 2020 Published by American Society

The flow of fertilizer with the irrigation stream is a special case
page=2 block=1842943543 (x=289 y=221) width=240 height=5 fontname=LPPLBM+AdvOT483a8203 wordint.min=1 wordint.max=5
of advective-dispersive transport. Advection refers to the move-
page=2 block=1842943543 (x=277 y=232) width=252 height=6 fontname=LPPLBM+AdvOT483a8203 wordint.min=1 wordint.max=6
ment of solutes with the mean velocity of the conveying fluid,
page=2 block=1842943543 (x=277 y=244) width=252 height=5 fontname=LPPLBM+AdvOT483a8203 wordint.min=1 wordint.max=6
and thus as a result of the mean forces acting on that flow, whereas
page=2 block=1842943543 (x=277 y=255) width=252 height=5 fontname=LPPLBM+AdvOT483a8203 wordint.min=1 wordint.max=4
dispersion considers the transport and mixing of the solute as a re-
page=2 block=1842943543 (x=277 y=266) width=252 height=6 fontname=LPPLBM+AdvOT483a8203 wordint.min=1 wordint.max=5
sult of velocity gradients within a cross section, flow turbulence,
page=2 block=1842943543 (x=277 y=278) width=252 height=5 fontname=LPPLBM+AdvOT483a8203 wordint.min=1 wordint.max=5
and molecular diffusion. Numerous studies have discussed the prin-
page=2 block=1842943543 (x=277 y=289) width=252 height=5 fontname=LPPLBM+AdvOT483a8203 wordint.min=1 wordint.max=4
ciples of advective-dispersive transport in open-channel water
page=2 block=1842943543 (x=277 y=300) width=252 height=6 fontname=LPPLBM+AdvOT483a8203 wordint.min=1 wordint.max=7
flows (e.g., Fischer et al. 1979; Cunge et al. 1980). The analysis
page=2 block=1842943543 (x=277 y=311) width=252 height=6 fontname=LPPLBM+AdvOT483a8203 wordint.min=1 wordint.max=6
assumes a neutrally buoyant solute and relatively small concentra-
page=2 block=1842943543 (x=277 y=323) width=252 height=5 fontname=LPPLBM+AdvOT483a8203 wordint.min=1 wordint.max=4
tions. These conditions imply that the solute has no effect on the
page=2 block=1842943543 (x=277 y=334) width=252 height=5 fontname=LPPLBM+AdvOT483a8203 wordint.min=1 wordint.max=5
density of the water and, consequently, on the irrigation flow. The
page=2 block=1842943543 (x=277 y=345) width=252 height=6 fontname=LPPLBM+AdvOT483a8203 wordint.min=1 wordint.max=5
analysis also assumes that the constituent is not subject to chemical
page=2 block=1842943543 (x=277 y=357) width=252 height=5 fontname=LPPLBM+AdvOT483a8203 wordint.min=1 wordint.max=4
reactions and that the only sink is the solute lost via infiltration. If
page=2 block=1842943543 (x=277 y=368) width=252 height=5 fontname=LPPLBM+AdvOT483a8203 wordint.min=1 wordint.max=5
the solute is applied in a pulse of short duration relative to the water
page=2 block=1842943543 (x=277 y=379) width=252 height=5 fontname=LPPLBM+AdvOT483a8203 wordint.min=1 wordint.max=4
flow event of interest, dispersion causes the concentration of the
page=2 block=1842943543 (x=277 y=390) width=252 height=6 fontname=LPPLBM+AdvOT483a8203 wordint.min=1 wordint.max=5
pulse to spread and attenuate as it travels downstream.

Solving the problem of solute advection-dispersion in surface
