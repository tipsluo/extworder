Classics in Chemical Neuroscience: Amitriptyline

==============================

type: abstract
typeindex=0 left=1 right=415 upper=146 lower=240
====>
charfont height=6.000000, charfont bold=0, alignment=-1, allupper=-1, column left=-1, likeBody=100

ABSTRACT: Amitriptyline was the second tricyclic antidepressant to appear on the market for major
depressive disorder under the brand name Elavil in 1961. Since its emergence, amitriptyline has been an
e?ective therapeutic in various disease states and disorders but has also been a concerning source of
cardiotoxicity. Amitriptyline inhibits serotonin and norepinephrine reuptake as well as produces o?-target
activity at histaminergic, muscarinic, and various other receptors. Its role as a modulator of monoamines
helped further establish the monoamine theory to understand various mood disorders, paving the way for the
now more common selective serotonin/norepinephrine reuptake inhibitors. In this review, we will discuss
amitriptyline’s synthesis, manufacturing information, drug metabolism, pharmacology, adverse e?ects, and its
history and importance in therapy to present amitriptyline as a true classic in chemical neuroscience.

that a?ects mood and cognition. It presents mainly as

from mild to severe, MDD’s diagnosis is based on a collection

include lethargy, appetite changes, suicidal ideation, among

approximately half of those diagnosed with MDD also present
with substance-abuse disorders. Comorbidity with anxiety
disorders or personality disorders occurs in roughly a third of
those a?ected by MDD.4?8

toms primarily through regulation of monoamines, speci?cally
serotonin and norepinephrine. Amitriptyline inhibits serotonin
and norepinephrine reuptake, though its primary metabolite,
nortriptyline, is a more potent inhibitor of norepinephrine
reuptake than serotonin. Amitriptyline inhibits the serotonin
reuptake transporter (SERT, Ki = 3?4 nM) and the

amitriptyline itself, though the 10-hydroxy metabolites (11,
12, 13, 14) are equipotent to amitriptyline in inhibiting
norepinephrine reuptake.36 These neurotransmitters and
respective receptors have been implicated in the symptomatol-

therapeutic picture, however. Growing evidence suggests
depression symptomatology is very polygenic. Neurotrophic
dysfunction and neuroimmune dysfunction appear to be
prominent possible causes as well.42,43 Brain-derived neuro-
trophic factor (BDNF) is a key player for the neurotrophic
hypothesis, promoting synaptogenesis and general neuro-
development.43,44

within certain areas of the brain; amitriptyline increases BDNF
mRNA expression but only in astrocytic and microglial

not appear to act directly on neurons, glial cell dysfunction and
atrophy can still be large contributing factors in MDD
neuropathophysiology.46 Amitriptyline may exert its therapeu-
tic e?ects both through the neurotrophic and through the
monoamine hypotheses for MDD.
